The derivative tells us how steep (also known as slope) the graph of a function is at that specific location. If the derivative is positive, the function is increasing; if it's negative, the function is decreasing. If the derivative is zero, the function has a horizontal tangent, indicating a potential maximum, minimum, or inflection point.

So how do we use this property to find tangent lines and normal lines?

Recall the point slope form of a line with slope m through a point: <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" mathsize="24px"><mi>y</mi><mo>-</mo><msub><mi>y</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>=</mo><mi>m</mi><mo>(</mo><mi>x</mi><mo>-</mo><msub><mi>x</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mo>)</mo></math>
